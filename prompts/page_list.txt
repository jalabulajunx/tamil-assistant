You are helping a 10-year-old Indian-origin Canadian boy read his Tamil Nadu primary-school Tamil textbook. He reads English like a 14-year-old, is learning French at school, and has limited Tamil. His grandmother teaches him.

List every Tamil word visible on this page (printed or handwritten), in reading order. List each distinct word once.

For each word give:
- "w": the word in Tamil script, exactly as on the page
- "en": a short English meaning, 1-4 words
- "rom": ISO 15919 romanization (e.g. "viṭai", "tamiḻ")
- "say": how to say it using English letters, syllables separated by hyphens, following these rules:
  - long vowels doubled: aa (ஆ), ee (ஈ), oo (ஊ); ஏ = ay, ஓ = oh, ஐ = ai, ஔ = au
  - CAPITAL letters mean "curl the tongue back": T or D for ட, N for ண, L for ள
  - zh for ழ; R for the trilled ற; r for ர; ற்ற is said "ttr"
  - th for த (soft, tongue touching the teeth); n for ந and ன
  - write sounds as a Tamil speaker actually says them (e.g. ப between vowels sounds like b)
  Examples: விடை = "vi-Dai", தமிழ் = "tha-mizh", வணக்கம் = "va-Nak-kam", பள்ளி = "paL-Li"

If the page is mostly pictures with few or no words, add one entry with "w": "PICTURE_NOTE" and "en": a 1-2 sentence description of what the pictures show and the story they suggest.
If the page has a poem or song, end with one entry with "w": "POEM_SUMMARY" and "en": a 2-3 sentence summary of its meaning for a 10-year-old.

Return only a JSON array of objects with the keys w, en, rom, say.
